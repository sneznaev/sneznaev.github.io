Privacy Policy

What data Timespan uses and where it goes.

Effective date: November 26, 2026

Timespan is an Android app that tracks how you spend your time and shows you statistics. It is developed by Sergei Neznaev (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what data the app uses and where it goes.

The short version

  • Everything you record in Timespan stays on your phone. We don't run a server, and we can't see your data.
  • There's no sign-up and no account.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob. Google's ad library collects some device data on its own — see below.
  • If the app crashes, it sends us a technical crash report through Google Firebase Crashlytics — with none of your records in it. You can turn this off in the app's settings.

Your records

Your activities, times, settings, and the pictures and photos you pick for icons are stored inside the app on your phone. They aren't sent anywhere — not to us, not to anyone else.

Android cloud backup is turned off for Timespan. The only way to move your data to another phone is a backup file: you save it wherever you choose (phone storage, a cloud drive, and so on) and open it yourself. We have no access to that file.

If you pick your own sound file for background noise, the app can only read that one file and plays it on your phone — it doesn't copy it or send it anywhere.

Ads

To keep the app free, it shows a Google AdMob banner. Google's ad library (Google Mobile Ads SDK) may collect the following and share it with Google:

  • your device's advertising ID and app set ID;
  • your IP address and the approximate location it reveals;
  • device and app details (model, Android version, language);
  • app launches, ad views and taps;
  • diagnostics, such as app launch time, hangs and battery use.

Google uses this data to serve ads, measure them, prevent fraud and, if you agree, show ads based on your interests. The data goes to Google and, if you agree, to Google's advertising partners (in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the consent form lists them). Nothing you record in Timespan is ever shared with advertisers.

How Google uses this data: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Consent. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent form when it's needed — usually on first launch. You can change your choice later in Settings → About → Privacy settings.

Limiting interest-based ads. In Android settings, go to Google → Ads, where you can delete or reset your advertising ID (exact menu names vary by phone). You can also manage ad personalization for your Google Account at myadcenter.google.com.

Crash reports

When the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics (a Google service) sends us a crash report so we can find and fix the bug. A report contains:

  • where in the code the crash happened (the stack trace) and the app's state at that moment;
  • device details: model, brand, Android version;
  • a random installation ID that Crashlytics creates to count how many people a crash affects.

A report never contains your activities, times or anything else you record. It's sent over an encrypted connection, and Google processes it on our behalf only to show us the crash.

Crash reports are on by default. To turn them off, go to Settings → About → Send crash reports; the change takes effect the next time you open the app.

Permissions

The app only asks for what its features need, and all of it works on your phone:

  • Notifications — the ongoing notification with your current activity, and reminders.
  • Alarms and starting after a reboot — so Pomodoro and reminders go off on time.
  • Vibration — to signal a Pomodoro phase change.
  • Do Not Disturb access — only if you turn it on for focus sessions.
  • Background playback — background noise during focus, if you turn it on.
  • Internet — for ads and crash reports.
  • Advertising ID — for ads only.

Photos for icons are picked with the system photo picker: the app only sees the photo you choose and keeps a copy of it on your phone.

Storage, security and deletion

Your records are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read. The backup file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere only you can open. To delete everything, uninstall the app or clear its data in Android settings; backup files you've saved stay wherever you put them. We don't store any of your data ourselves, except crash reports: they're kept in our Firebase project and deleted under Google's rules. Data collected by Google's ad library is kept and deleted under Google's own rules (see the link above).

Your rights

You can ask what data we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, and turn off crash reports. We don't hold your records — they live only on your phone, and only you can delete them. Crash reports don't include your name or email, so we usually can't tell which ones are yours, but write to us and we'll do what we can. Ad data is handled by Google: change your choice in the app's Privacy settings or at myadcenter.google.com. If you live in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, you also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

The app isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect children's data.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. If the app ever starts collecting anything else, we'll say so here first.
